The internal knowledge base

I centralized technical articles in Confluence so IT support staff had a shared place to look up procedures. I worked with security, infrastructure, and data teams on the documentation they contributed.

My contribution

I set the standards for articles and coached one technical writer. I also assigned and prioritized that writer’s work and reviewed and approved the drafts.

  • Article structure: standardized how articles were organized so support staff could scan them consistently.
  • Review and ownership: set expectations for who maintained content and how it was reviewed and revised.
  • Change tracking: used Jira alongside Confluence to track revisions.

Designing for the next revision

A useful knowledge base has two audiences: the person who needs an answer now, and the person responsible for keeping that answer current.

I use those two needs to guide the structure: can someone find the procedure during a support request, and can a contributor tell who needs to review it when the system changes?

The Amherst knowledge base was for internal IT support staff.
