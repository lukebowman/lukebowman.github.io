Approach note / Reporting & information design

Designing operational reports

How I think about organizing a report: start with the reader’s question, make comparisons understandable, and keep the interpretation close to the evidence.

In this note

The reader’s taskMy approachDesign principlesContact

What the reader needs

A report can contain the right numbers and still be difficult to use. Readers need to know what is being measured, what the comparison means, and where to look for supporting detail.

Overview and supporting detail

I favor a concise overview followed by progressively more detail. Clear headings, consistent labels, and explanations near the relevant figures make the document easier to navigate without asking the reader to reconstruct the story.

Reporting checklist

Define the comparison

Name the reporting period, define the measures, and explain the basis of comparison. A number becomes more useful when its context is explicit.

Group related measures

Organize related measures into recognizable categories. Give readers a clear overview before asking them to examine individual details.

Separate observations from explanations

Use conventional axes, direct labels, and visible caveats. Explain what the data supports and distinguish observation from a proposed explanation.
